This task force establishes the foundational context for the Working Group by addressing motivation, scope, and reference case studies. While established engineering practices often rely on historical climate records and the assumption of stationarity, the reality of a changing climate introduces environmental actions that can no longer be treated as constant over the service life of a structure. We aim to present where existing reliability frameworks fall short, and to delineate the specific climate variables, structural systems, and adaptation strategies that define this new engineering reality. By bridging the gap between climate science and structural engineering, we seek to provide a rigorous basis for decision-making under deep uncertainty.

Our work follows three steps: (1) a comprehensive review of existing codes and standards to identify how - or if - current regulatory frameworks account for time-dependent climate hazards; (2) a structured definition of scope that categorizes climate variables, structure types, and the multi-layered uncertainties inherent in future projections; and (3) the selection of reference case studies. These cases, ranging from coastal defenses to building portfolios, serve as shared benchmarks for the entire task force. They ensure that new methodological developments are grounded in representative engineering problems to evaluate structural reliability and performance across diverse climatic scenarios and infrastructure assets.
