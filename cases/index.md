This task force establishes the foundational context for the Working Group by addressing motivation, scope, and reference case studies. While established engineering practices often rely on historical climate records and the assumption of stationarity, the reality of a changing climate introduces environmental actions that can no longer be treated as constant over the service life of a structure. We aim to establish a shared technical basis for the Task Force by articulating why the existing framework for structural reliability is insufficient in the face of climate change, delineating what will be addressed in terms of climate variables, structural systems, and failure mechanisms, and proposing a set of reference case studies to be used as common benchmarks by the other subgroups and working groups.

Our work follows three steps: (1) a review of existing codes and standards to identify how - or if - current regulatory frameworks account for time-dependent climate hazards; (2) a definition of scope that categorizes climate variables, structure types, and the multi-layered uncertainties inherent in future projections; and (3) the selection of reference case studies. These cases, ranging from coastal defenses to building portfolios, serve as shared benchmarks for the entire task force.
