Climate conditions are changing significantly over time and are subject to substantial uncertainty, originating from the trajectory of future global emissions, the structural and parametric limitations of climate models, and the possibility of tipping points that may lead to abrupt and severe shifts. Consequently, the environmental actions relevant to structural design can no longer reasonably be treated as stationary over the service life of a structure. Current practice, however, continues to rely largely on historical climate records and on an implicit assumption of stationarity, so that structures designed today may fail to meet the requirements and societal expectations for safety, reliability, resilience, and sustainability by the end of their intended service lives.

How to handle climate change effects in the management of risk and safety of structures and infrastructure? 
How to sustainably deal with uncertainty in hazards and extremes in the built environment affected by climate change?

The Working Group on Uncertain Climate Futures prepares the scientific background to enhance policies, standards and rules for infrastructures and structures. 

The working group operates under the auspieces of the Joint Committee on Structural Safety (JCSS).
