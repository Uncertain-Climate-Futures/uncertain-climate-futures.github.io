We develop a methodological framework for the design and assessment of structures and infrastructure under uncertain climate futures. Its starting point is probability-based reliability and risk analysis, which provides the established basis for rational engineering decisions. Climate change, however, poses new challenges: loads become inherently non-stationary, exposure and vulnerability evolve, and future pathways may be difficult to describe through a single probabilistic model. 

We investigate sequential decision-making under uncertainty, with the aim of identifying pragmatic methods, algorithms, and metrics for theoretically sound and practically useful design and assessment decisions under climate change. It examines how probabilistic modelling can be applied, tested, and complemented in this setting. Attention will also be devoted to deep uncertainty and to non-probabilistic approaches developed in the climate-change community. Rather than contrasting probabilistic and non-probabilistic methods, we aim to explore how they can be connected within a coherent decision framework.
